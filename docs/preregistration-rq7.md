# Preregistration: Can a human reader detect the rubric's signal, and the readers' signal, in an opening chapter? (RQ7, round 2)

**Study:** Reliable but Invalid: a criterion-validity test of LLM-as-judge evaluation
**Repository:** https://github.com/XZ-LI/llm-rubric-validity
**Author and sole annotator:** Xuezhao Li
**Status at registration:** The round-2 annotation materials were generated on
2026-11-25. No round-2 item has been viewed or answered. The answer key has not been
opened by the annotator.

---

## 1. Background

In the main study, a genre-specific rubric scored by `qwen3-max` showed high test–retest
reliability (ICC(1,1) = .874), but its validity against platform reader behaviour was
negligible (within-cell standardized r = .138 against exposure-normalized bookmark rate,
n = 768). Two explanations remain open:

- **(A) The rubric is invalid.** It measures something readers do not respond to.
- **(B) The criterion is noisy.** Platform behaviour is shaped by recommendation,
  exposure, covers and titles, so no text-based judgment could predict it well.

A human forced choice between two opening chapters is a criterion that does not pass
through the platform. But a hit rate of 50% on rubric-contrasting pairs is ambiguous on
its own: the rubric may carry no signal, or the annotator may be unable to tell any two
2,000-character openings apart. This design therefore runs two conditions side by side.

**Condition R (positive control).** The two books differ clearly in reader rating, but
the rubric scores them almost equally. It tests whether the annotator can detect the
*readers'* signal in this window at all.

**Condition S (validity test).** The two books differ clearly in rubric score, but readers
rate them almost equally. It tests whether the side the *rubric* prefers is the side a
human would rather keep reading.

## 2. Hypotheses

**H1 (confirmatory, positive control).** In condition R, the annotator chooses the book
with the higher reader rating more often than chance.

**H2 (confirmatory, validity).** In condition S, the annotator's rate of choosing the
rubric's higher-scoring book differs from chance.

**H3 (secondary).** The hit rate in R is higher than the hit rate in S.

## 3. Design

### 3.1 Materials

- **Source:** the 840-novel held-out set (`public/corpus_manifest.json`), restricted to the
  `qwen3-max` scoring run (`validation_pilot.json`, one per genre × channel cell).
- **Exclusions before sampling:** per-cell contamination exclusions from
  `src/contamination.py` (`excluded_files`): every text used in rubric induction, judge
  calibration, or the generator's prompt. Texts from an earlier, abandoned annotation
  tool are not excluded (§3.8).
- **Excerpt:** the first 2,000 characters of chapter 1, the same window the LLM judge saw.
  Texts whose chapter 1 is shorter than 600 characters are dropped.
- **Reader rating:** the platform rating (`real_rating`), as listed in the manifest.
- **Rubric score:** the `qwen3-max` rubric score (`rubric_score`).

### 3.2 Pair construction

Generated by `annotation/build_annotation_round2.py`, seed `20261022`, with
`--allow-reused --rating-pairs 120 --rubric-pairs 50`. All pairs are within a single
cell. No text appears in more than one pair.

| | Condition R (positive control) | Condition S (validity test) |
|---|---|---|
| Reader rating gap | ≥ 1.0 | ≤ 0.3 |
| Rubric score gap | ≤ 0.5 | ≥ 1.5 |
| "High" side | higher reader rating | higher rubric score |
| Pairs requested | 120 (i.e. every pair available) | 50 |
| Pairs obtained | **89** | **50** |

R was requested above supply so that every qualifying pair is used: only 89 exist under
these thresholds. Within each condition, candidate pairs in each cell are ranked by contrast (largest
first). Cells are visited in random order, one pair per cell per pass, up to 4 pairs per
cell, so no genre dominates. Left/right position is randomized per pair. R pairs, S pairs
and attention checks are shuffled together.

The answer key contains **89 R pairs, 50 S pairs and 6 attention checks (145 items)**.
Generating it twice with the same settings gave the same file. Its SHA-256 is:

```
8c833ac708416cb2f0b93f2b9e677367b07f7611932e7ffbb0f18d44c96a2813  annotation/round2_key.json
```

The key file is published after annotation, and its hash can be checked against this
registration.

### 3.3 Attention checks

Six check items: the same excerpt on both sides, with the sentence order scrambled on one
side (first and last sentences kept in place). The intact side is the correct answer.
These verify that the annotator is reading. They do not test discrimination; condition R
does.

### 3.4 Blinding

- The annotation page shows only the two excerpts. Hidden: title, author, reader rating,
  reader counts, rubric score, cell, condition, and which side is "high".
- **The answer key is a separate file** (`round2_key.json`). The page contains no
  answers, so it cannot score itself or reveal anything through its source code.
- The annotator cannot tell R items from S items, so both conditions are answered under
  the same state and on the same days.
- No score is shown at any point, including after the last item.

### 3.5 Task

Prompt (verbatim): **这两个开头，你更想接着读哪一个？** ("Which of these two openings would
you rather keep reading?"). The instruction asks for the one the annotator would rather
continue reading, not the "better" one. The construct is **continuation intention**.

For each pair the annotator may also tick **"这一对我基本是猜的"** ("I was mostly guessing
on this pair"). Ticking it does not remove the pair (§5.3).

### 3.6 Annotator

A single annotator: the author, a native Chinese reader familiar with the genre. **The
annotator also authored the rubric's seed dimensions**, so any bias should favour the
rubric in condition S. A null result in S is therefore conservative. The study is
reported as a single-expert annotation, not a reader study.

### 3.7 Prior expectations

Recorded here before registration, and again in the tool's sealed prompt before the
first item:

> Condition R (reader-rating contrast): predicted hit rate **60%**.
> Condition S (rubric contrast): predicted hit rate **50%**.

### 3.8 Earlier, abandoned annotation

A first annotation tool (`annotation/build_annotation.py`, 111 rubric-contrasting pairs,
seed `20260921`) was generated in October 2026. It was answered casually, without close
reading, on 31 items, and then abandoned in favour of this design because it had no
positive control. Those answers are **not analysed and will not be reported as
evidence**. Because those texts were not read closely, they are not excluded from this
round. Any text the annotator recognizes is handled by exclusion rule 2 (§4).

A draft of this round, generated on 2026-10-23, excluded all 222 first-tool texts and
yielded 70 R pairs. It was replaced before any item was viewed, to increase the number of
R pairs.

## 4. Data exclusion

Decided before data collection and applied before any outcome is computed. Rules 2 and 3
are implemented in `annotation/analyze_annotation.py`.

1. **Batch rejection.** If 2 or more of the 6 attention checks are failed, the whole batch
   is discarded and not analysed. A recognized check (rule 2) counts as neither passed
   nor failed.
2. **Recognized books.** If the annotator recognizes either book in a pair (title, plot or
   characters), they press the skip key instead of choosing. The pair is recorded as
   `recognized` and excluded. The count is reported.
3. **Response time.** Pairs answered in under **20 seconds** are excluded as not read.
   Time is measured from the previous answer, or from page load for the first pair of a
   session. The count is reported.
4. No other exclusions. Pairs are not excluded based on the choice, the cell, the contrast
   or the "guessing" mark.

## 5. Analysis

The confirmatory analysis is produced by running `annotation/analyze_annotation.py` on
the round-2 export **only**, passed explicitly as an argument. The script also looks for
earlier exports when run without arguments; that output is not used.

### 5.1 H1 and H2 (confirmatory)

- **Unit:** pair. **Outcome:** hit = the annotator chose the "high" side for that
  condition (§3.2).
- **Test:** exact two-sided binomial test against p = .5, α = .05, separately for R and S.
- **Reported:** hits, n, hit rate, exact p, and Wilson 95% confidence interval.
- H1 and H2 answer different questions, and each is interpreted on its own (§7), so no
  correction for multiple comparisons is applied.

### 5.2 H3 (secondary)

Difference between R and S hit rates, two-proportion z-test, two-sided, α = .05.

### 5.3 Secondary analyses (pre-specified)

- **By confidence:** hit rates for R and S, split by the "guessing" mark.
- **Dose–response:** within each condition, pairs split into tertiles by contrast;
  Cochran–Armitage trend test on hit rate across tertiles.

### 5.4 Exploratory analyses (labelled as exploratory if reported)

- Whether the annotator's choices predict within-cell bookmark rate.
- Hit rates by channel (男频 / 女频) and by cell (descriptive only).
- Agreement with additional annotators, if collected later (separate registration).

## 6. Sample size and power

The sample size is fixed by the supply of pairs meeting the criteria in §3.2, not by a
power target. Two-sided exact binomial test, α = .05:

| | n | Significant at | Power at 60% | 65% | 70% | 75% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R | 89 | ≥ 55 hits (61.8%) | .41 | .77 | .96 | >.99 |
| S | 50 | ≥ 33 hits (66.0%) | .24 | .51 | .78 | .94 |

If the hit rate is 50%, the 95% CI is roughly [40%, 60%] for R and [37%, 63%] for S.

**Condition R is also underpowered at the annotator's predicted rate.** If the true R hit
rate is 60% (§3.7), the test has about a 41% chance of reaching significance, and the
annotation would then be classed as uninformative (§7). This is accepted as a limit of a
single-annotator design.

**Condition S is underpowered for small effects.** It can reliably detect only a strong
rubric signal (around 70% or more). A non-significant S result is reported as "no
evidence of a hit rate above roughly 63–66%", with its confidence interval, and not as
proof that the rubric has no signal. H3 has about 65% power when R = 70% and S = 50%.

## 7. Inference criteria

| | S not different from 50% | S above 50% |
|---|---|---|
| **R above 50%** | **The annotator can read the readers' signal but not the rubric's.** Strongest support for (A) | The rubric carries part of the readers' signal. The main null needs to be reconsidered |
| **R not different from 50%** | **The annotator cannot discriminate in this window.** The human annotation is uninformative and is not used for any conclusion | Rubric signal without reader signal: reported as is, with no further interpretation |

If S is significantly **below** 50%, it is reported as a reversal.

The bottom-left cell is the key safeguard. If the positive control fails, neither a null
nor a positive result in S is interpreted.

## 8. Known limitations

- **Single annotator.** Results describe one expert reader, not readers in general.
- **Not blind to the hypothesis.** The annotator knows the study and wrote the rubric's
  seed dimensions. The sealed predictions (§3.7) are recorded so expectation effects can
  be discussed.
- **Prior exposure.** The annotator may have read some texts during the project. Handled
  by exclusion rule 2.
- **Reader rating as the R criterion.** Platform rating is only one of the six behavioural
  criteria in the main study, and not the primary one (bookmark rate). R tests
  discrimination against reader rating specifically.
- **Response time** includes any pause taken mid-pair, so only a lower bound is used.
- **S has limited power** (§6).
